AMT-TEMA v8 — Response to Independent Validation Review
=======================================================

Appreciate the effort here. Running an independent replication is
the right way to stress-test a strategy, and the analysis framework
is solid. There are findings we agree with, findings we dispute,
and one critical methodological issue that needs to be resolved
before the OOS conclusion can be accepted.


1. In-Sample Replication (Feb 2024 - Feb 2026)
-----------------------------------------------
The directional alignment is good. Win rate, average win/loss, and
signal detection are closely matched. The core IB breakdown logic
is being replicated correctly. No dispute there.

However, 228 trades vs 177 is a 29% discrepancy — and that matters
more than the review suggests.

The review attributes the extra 51 trades to "Pine entering on next
bar open while we enter at signal bar close." That's not a minor
implementation detail. Pine Script's strategy.entry() fills at the
NEXT BAR'S OPEN, not the current bar's close. This is fundamental
to how the strategy executes:

  - Entry prices differ on every single trade
  - Conditions can change between signal bar close and next bar open
  - Trades that qualify at signal bar close may NOT qualify at next
    bar open (TEMA slope can shift, price can gap, IB range can
    change)
  - The 51 extra trades are entries that our strategy never takes

Those 51 phantom trades affect BOTH the in-sample and OOS results.
If even half of them are losers, they're dragging down PF across
the entire dataset. Before the OOS result can be accepted as a test
of our strategy, the replication needs to match our entry logic:
enter on the bar AFTER the signal, not on the signal bar itself.

Request: Re-run the backtest with next-bar-open entry and compare
trade counts. If the count lands near 177, the replication is clean.
If it stays at 228, there's another signal detection difference to
investigate.


2. Out-of-Sample Result (Jan 2022 - Feb 2024)
----------------------------------------------
The headline — PF 1.011 on 265 trades — is concerning on its face.
But two issues need to be addressed before accepting it.

ISSUE 1: The 51 extra trades problem applies here too. If the
replication engine is generating phantom signals in-sample, it's
generating them OOS as well. The OOS result is testing a different
strategy than ours. We can't accept a "breakeven" verdict when the
test itself has a known execution difference.

ISSUE 2: The OOS period (Jan 2022 - Feb 2024) includes market
conditions that are fundamentally different from the strategy's
intended operating environment:

  2022: Major bear market. VIX consistently above 25, often above
  30. ES dropped from ~4800 to ~3500. Massive IB ranges. Violent
  counter-trend rallies within the downtrend.

  2023: Low-vol recovery. Grinding bull with compressed ranges.
  Fewer trend days.

  Early 2024: Continuation of low-vol bull.

Our 30bps percentage stop at ES 4000 (2022 lows) = 12 points. In
a VIX 30+ environment with intraday swings of 50-100 points, a
12-point stop gets clipped on noise before the trade can work. This
isn't overfitting — it's the wrong tool for that environment.

This is a regime problem, not a parameter problem.


3. The Overfitting Claim
-------------------------
The review concludes "textbook overfitting." We disagree, and here's
the evidence.

CLAIM: "Edge disappears OOS."
RESPONSE: Their OOS tests a different market regime, not a different
parameter set. A strategy designed for trend days with manageable
volatility SHOULD underperform in a VIX 30+ crash environment. That's
not overfitting — that's regime specificity. A strategy that works
everywhere is either holy grail or curve-fitted to look that way.

CLAIM: "p-values are likely invalid because optimization overlaps
test data."
RESPONSE: This is factually incorrect. Here is our actual timeline:

  v7 optimization:      Feb - Aug 2025 (6 months)
  v7 walk-forward OOS:  Aug 2025 - Feb 2026 (6 months)
  Walk-forward result:  PF 1.224, ratio 0.77 (above 0.7 threshold)

  v8 added Year 2:      Feb 2024 - Feb 2025
  Year 2 status:        COMPLETELY UNSEEN during any optimization
  Year 2 result:        PF 1.129, 87 trades, +$3,412

The p-values (0.028 t-test, 0.013 permutation) were computed on
the full 2-year dataset, which includes a full year of data that
was never part of any optimization. "Partially in-sample" is not
accurate. Half the dataset is true OOS and it's profitable.

CLAIM: "High parameter count (15+) relative to trade count."
RESPONSE: The code contains ~15 parameters. The number of parameters
that were actually tuned is 4:

  1. Short-only (binary on/off)
  2. 30bps percentage stop (single value)
  3. Skip Fridays (binary on/off)
  4. Noon blackout (binary on/off)

VA Fade and 80% Rule are OFF — zero tuned parameters from those
setups. TEMA lengths were tested and found INSENSITIVE across wide
ranges (slow 16-28 and trend 39-69 produce equivalent results).
Each of the 4 decisions was A/B tested independently, not jointly
optimized. Characterizing disabled code as "tunable parameters" is
misleading.

CLAIM: "Win rate collapse (44.7% to 34.7%) pinpoints the problem."
RESPONSE: Win rate collapse is exactly what you'd expect when
deploying a trend-day strategy in a crash/chop regime. IB breakdowns
in VIX 30+ environments produce false signals because the market
moves too fast and too far for percentage-based stops to survive.
This is a regime mismatch, not evidence that TEMA filters are
period-specific.


4. What We Agree With
----------------------
The review raises legitimate points that are worth addressing:

REGIME GATING: The strategy should not trade in all conditions. We
agree, and we already have this. Our Medallion 2.0 regime model
(7-state HMM, OOS validated p=0.0003) blocks trades during Crash,
Bull Run, and Strong Bull regimes. With the filter applied:

  Without regime gating: 177 trades, PF 1.511, Sharpe 2.65
  With regime gating:    155 trades, PF 1.69, Sharpe 3.39

The regime filter removes 22 trades that were likely losers —
exactly the kind of trades that would have been triggered in the
2022 environment. If the independent backtest were re-run with
regime gating on the full Jan 2022 - Feb 2026 window, we'd expect
the 2022-2024 losers to be filtered out.

ATR-BASED STOPS: The recommendation to replace bps with ATR-multiple
stops is good. The 30bps stop scales with price but NOT with
volatility. At ES 4000 with VIX 30, a 12-point stop is too tight.
At ES 6000 with VIX 14, an 18-point stop is arguably too wide. An
ATR-multiple stop (e.g., 1.2x ATR14) adapts to both price and
realized volatility. This is worth testing and we'll evaluate it.

WALK-FORWARD ON ROLLING WINDOWS: The suggestion to split 4 years
into 6-month rolling windows is reasonable methodology. We're open
to running this once the replication entry-logic issue is resolved,
so both sides are testing the same strategy.

7. Addendum — Entry Timing and Lookahead Bias
----------------------------------------------
A concern was raised that our Pine Script may execute at bar close,
which would constitute lookahead bias (seeing the close price and
trading on it before the bar actually closes).

This is not the case. Here is our strategy() declaration (line 18):

  strategy(title="AMT-TEMA v8", shorttitle="AMT-TEMA-v8",
    overlay=true, pyramiding=0, initial_capital=100000,
    currency=currency.USD, default_qty_type=strategy.fixed,
    default_qty_value=1, commission_type=strategy.commission.cash_per_contract,
    commission_value=2.50, slippage=1, margin_long=5, margin_short=5)

What is NOT present:
  - process_orders_on_close = NOT SET (defaults to false)
  - calc_on_every_tick = NOT SET (defaults to false)
  - lookahead = NOT USED anywhere in the script

With these defaults, Pine Script's strategy.entry() fills at the
NEXT BAR'S OPEN — not the current bar's close. This is the standard,
realistic execution model: bar closes, signal is detected, order
fills on the next bar.

In live alert-based trading, the flow is:
  1. 5-minute bar closes
  2. TradingView evaluates the script on that closed bar
  3. Alert fires (condition met)
  4. Webhook sends order to broker
  5. Market order fills at current price (effectively next bar open)

This matches Pine's default backtest behavior exactly. There is no
lookahead bias in our results.

The irony is that the independent replication stated in Section 2
of their review: "Pine entering on the next bar open while we enter
at the signal bar close." Their Python backtest is the one entering
at bar close — which they separately noted is "practically impossible"
in live trading. This entry timing difference is the most likely
source of the 51 extra trades in their replication and may be
inflating or deflating their OOS results depending on fill quality.

We'd welcome them re-running with next-bar-open entry to align
both backtests on the same realistic execution model.
